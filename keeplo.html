Phase 01: Subscription Diagnostics

1. Executive Summary & Core Diagnostics

This strategic briefing provides a comprehensive evaluation of the subscription retention architecture developed for Keeplo, establishing a data-verified baseline to guide executive decision-making. By auditing the transactional ledger through a rigorous analytics pipeline, this report translates raw operational database logs into high-level business intelligence to address critical customer attrition points.

The analytical framework rests on the core institutional mandate that protecting recurring revenue from the existing subscriber base is fundamentally more profitable than expanding front-end marketing acquisition funnels.

1.1 Core Financial and Operational Diagnostics

The baseline corporate metrics for Keeplo indicate a stabilized Monthly Recurring Revenue of $95,285 sustained by an active subscriber base of 1,789 users. While these indicators demonstrate solid market traction, corporate profitability is heavily constrained by a monthly Churn Rate of 27.5%, which accounts for a net loss of 568 customers within the current reporting cycle. This severe volume leak indicates that while customer acquisition pipelines are successfully converting initial demand, the platform faces an immediate utility bottleneck that compounds Customer Lifetime Value degradation.

The underlying revenue ledger proves that capital generation remains heavily dependent on the premium subscription tier which accounts for 72% of total contract volume, leaving the remaining 28% distributed across basic contracts. Consequently, stabilizing retention within the high-value premium segment represents the single most critical operational priority to mitigate compounding monthly revenue contraction.

Dissecting Cross-Departmental Operational Blind Spots

During the 2025-2026 expansion campaign, aggressive front-end acquisition masks an unsustainable operational pathology. While massive user registration spikes break historical records, they enter a pipeline where onboarding mechanisms fail to establish product adoption. Executive leadership remains divided on the root causes: the Product team suspects low-quality promotional leads, while Marketing asserts usability deficiencies, and Support experiences a breakdown under surging ticket logs. This framework removes subjective opinions, diagnosing systemic attrition through empirical data lineage.

1.2 Systemic Revenue Leakage Analysis

An evaluation of behavioral customer archetypes exposes a critical risk distribution within the user lifecycle matrix. The Recency, Frequency, and Monetary (RFM) calculation model isolates a matching mathematical spread where 40.83% of total unique accounts are classified as New or Recent Active users, while an identical 40.83% have already deteriorated into the At Risk category. This equilibrium exposes a severe structural breakdown immediately following the initial conversion funnel, where onboarding mechanisms fail to transition new signups into sustained platform adoption.

This operational deficit is validated by the transactional logs, which identify voluntary product abandonment due to a lack of core feature usage as the primary threat to revenue stability. This specific churn category accounts for 2,948 customer cancellations, completely eclipsing price-sensitive attrition which accounts for 965 cancellations, proving that customer loss is driven by onboarding failure rather than market pricing pressures.

1.3 Data Engineering Integrity and Mitigation Blueprint

To guarantee absolute operational clarity for leadership, the insights presented in this report originate from a fully audited data infrastructure layer designed to eliminate data pipeline noise and transactional anomalies. The production dbt Core pipeline successfully countered critical upstream quality deficits, executing the deletion of duplicate account records, the removal of negative accounting errors, and the normalization of fragmented contract fields that previously obscured true revenue distribution.

With data integrity established, reversing the 27.5% churn threshold requires an immediate two-pronged operational strategy. The product team must deploy automated feature activation protocols targeted at the 40.83% At Risk user pool to stimulate engagement, while the engineering team must integrate automated dunning management into the billing gateway to neutralize credit card expirations and bank rejections, which currently account for a combined total of 866 preventable customer cancellations.

Phase 03: Analytical Transformation

3. Data Architecture & Information Engineering

Raw transactional records and behavioral user logs are inherently chaotic, containing structural anomalies and reporting noise that can distort high-level executive metrics if left unremediated. To prevent strategic misalignment, a robust data pipeline was constructed using dbt Core, Polars, and DuckDB to ingest, clean, and model data from core operational databases into highly optimized analytics assets.

3.1 Data Lineage and Structural Source Mapping

The data architecture relies on three primary pipelines capturing customer relationship logs: account demographics (stg_customers), ledger transactions (stg_transactions), and platform activity systems (stg_user_activity). Through the analytics engineering layer, these layers compile into an optimized star schema framework designed to surface behavioral metrics.

3.2 Upstream Cleanup and Deduplication Pipeline

To ensure unique primary key constraints, the staging script deploys an elite analytics architecture, utilizing an elastically chained TRY_CAST structure to handle date anomalies and a high-performance QUALIFY layer to strip out duplicate records in a single execution sweep: 

-- stg_customers.sql Ingestion Architecture Framework
WITH source_data AS (
    SELECT * FROM {{ ref('raw_customers') }}
),

cleaned_customers AS (
    SELECT
        customer_id,
        
        -- The definitive elastic parser shield: TRY_CAST handles multi-format fields
        COALESCE(
            TRY_CAST(signup_date AS DATE),
            TRY_CAST(
                SPLIT_PART(signup_date, '/', 3) || '-' || 
                LPAD(SPLIT_PART(signup_date, '/', 1), 2, '0') || '-' || 
                LPAD(SPLIT_PART(signup_date, '/', 2), 2, '0') 
                AS DATE
            ),
            TRY_CAST(REPLACE(signup_date, '.', '-') AS DATE)
        ) AS signup_date,
        
        COALESCE(acquisition_channel, 'Unknown') AS acquisition_channel,
        UPPER(SUBSTRING(plan_type, 1, 1)) || LOWER(SUBSTRING(plan_type, 2)) AS plan_type,
        discount_code
    FROM source_data
)

SELECT * FROM cleaned_customers
QUALIFY ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY customer_id ORDER BY signup_date) = 1

3.3 Algorithmic RFM Behavioral Mart Modeling

In the dimensional modeling tier (fct_customer_rfm.sql), raw continuous log history is compiled into statistical score windows via the NTILE(4) window function, grouping users into operational blocks based on the mathematical frequency of their platform adoption: 

-- fct_customer_rfm.sql Dimensional Score Generation
WITH customers AS (
    SELECT customer_id, signup_date, plan_type, acquisition_channel, discount_code
    FROM {{ ref('stg_customers') }}
),
activity AS (
    SELECT customer_id, COUNT(log_id) AS total_sessions, MAX(activity_date) AS last_activity_date
    FROM {{ ref('stg_user_activity') }}
    GROUP BY customer_id
),
transactions AS (
    SELECT customer_id, SUM(amount_paid) AS total_monetary
    FROM {{ ref('stg_transactions') }}
    GROUP BY customer_id
),
user_metrics AS (
    SELECT
        c.customer_id, c.signup_date, c.plan_type, c.acquisition_channel, c.discount_code,
        DATE_DIFF('day', COALESCE(a.last_activity_date, c.signup_date), (SELECT MAX(signup_date) FROM customers)) AS recency_days,
        COALESCE(a.total_sessions, 0) AS frequency_count,
        COALESCE(t.total_monetary, 0.0) AS monetary_value
    FROM customers c
    LEFT JOIN activity a ON c.customer_id = a.customer_id
    LEFT JOIN transactions t ON c.customer_id = t.customer_id
),
rfm_scores AS (
    SELECT
        *,
        NTILE(4) OVER (ORDER BY recency_days DESC) AS r_score,
        NTILE(4) OVER (ORDER BY frequency_count ASC) AS f_score,
        NTILE(4) OVER (ORDER BY monetary_value ASC) AS m_score
    FROM user_metrics
)
SELECT 
    customer_id, signup_date, plan_type, acquisition_channel, discount_code,
    recency_days, frequency_count, monetary_value, r_score, f_score, m_score,
    CASE 
        WHEN r_score = 4 AND f_score = 4 THEN 'Champions (VIP)'
        WHEN r_score >= 3 AND f_score >= 3 THEN 'Loyal Customers'
        WHEN r_score >= 3 AND f_score <= 2 THEN 'New / Recent Active'
        WHEN r_score <= 2 AND f_score >= 3 THEN 'At Risk / Can''t Lose Them'
        ELSE 'Lost / Hibernating'
    END AS customer_segment
FROM rfm_scores
dbt Pipeline Quality Constraints

Remediating Structural Anomalies & Accounting Errors

Initial downstream database audits exposed systematic data corruption embedded within the source logs. The staging pipelines deployed rigid relational filtering to neutralize these quality deficits:

  • Ledger Filtering: Explicit eviction of negative accounting artifacts via WHERE amount_paid >= 0, removing severe MRR miscalculations.
  • Outlier Suppression: Execution of a constraint lock in the user log mapping layer to cast out corrupt metadata records showing absolute session lengths of 9,999 minutes.
  • Casing Normalization: Vectorized uppercase conversion on plan type attributes, collapsing fragmented textual variants into single clean dimensional elements.
Phase 04: Visual Engineering

4. Methodology & Technical Implementation in Tableau

This chapter outlines the methodological framework and analytical configurations executed within Tableau to transform the cleaned data infrastructure into an interactive corporate intelligence engine. Developing a corporate-grade dashboard requires moving beyond default software configurations to engineer customized data synchronization and advanced calculated dimensions.

4.1 Global Filter Synchronization and Interactive Architecture

The interactive architecture of the dashboard is designed to provide executive stakeholders with an intuitive drill-down experience without compromising visual alignment or data consistency. In a multi-source data environment, default analytical filters operate in isolation, which can lead to fragmented views and conflicting metrics across different charts. To eliminate this operational risk, the dashboard utilizes customized global scoping mechanics, ensuring that primary filters such as Subscription Month, Customer Segment, and Plan Type are structurally tied across independent data repositories.

This synchronization guarantees that when a director isolates a specific customer segment, the entire analytical canvas, including historical Monthly Recurring Revenue trends, core key performance indicators, and plan compositions, recalculates instantly. Furthermore, by locking all elements into absolute floating container coordinates with zero padding, the interface maintains its premium aesthetic integrity during high-volume query execution.

4.2 Advanced Analytical Engineering via Level of Detail Expressions

To capture the true operational reality of the customer lifecycle, the analytics layer integrates advanced Level of Detail expressions utilizing the FIXED syntax. Standard reporting tools calculate metrics based purely on what is visually displayed on the chart grid, which prevents the simultaneous evaluation of individual customer records against macro corporate baselines. The integration of advanced calculations overcomes this limitation by anchoring specific metrics directly to the unique customer identifier, completely independent of the visualization shelf dimensions.

This approach allowed for the successful deployment of the Recency, Frequency, and Monetary matrix, dividing users into precise behavioral blocks based on their true historical activity. Additionally, this methodology enabled the engineering of a dual-axis hybrid trend component, fusing high-density line graphics with translucent area shading to deliver premium interface aesthetics while accurately reflecting historical capital momentum.

Phase 06: Prescriptive Roadmap

6. Strategic Recommendations & Project Conclusions

To effectively reverse the current churn trajectory, management must deploy targeted interventions that directly address the specific failure modes exposed within the customer behavior ledger, converting data into prescriptive corporate execution.

6.1 Mitigation of Voluntary Attrition via Targeted Feature Engagement

Addressing the primary driver of subscription loss requires an immediate transformation of the post-conversion user journey, specifically targeting the 2,948 cancellations caused by a lack of platform utility. The product team should implement real-time monitoring triggers that detect a deceleration in core feature access within the first thirty days of subscription tenure. Upon detection of low usage anomalies, automated lifecycle marketing workflows must deliver personalized, context-specific feature discovery guides to demonstrate core value and accelerate product adoption.

6.2 Infrastructural Gateway Optimization for Involuntary Churn Remediation

To neutralize the 866 customer losses triggered by card failures, the engineering division must integrate an automated dunning management framework into the primary billing gateway, substituting blunt collection attempts with intelligent retry logic that schedules transaction retries based on optimal bank authorization windows. Furthermore, the implementation of an automated account updater service will securely synchronize expired card credentials with major financial institutions before a payment rejection occurs.

6.3 Future Work and Scalability Horizons

The unified dashboard addresses the core diagnostic deficit directly by providing a verified baseline of $95,285 in MRR. Evolving into subsequent operational cycles, the next technical phase will focus on migrating the production data pipeline into a fully automated cloud architecture, eliminating manual extraction steps and enabling real-time data ingestion. Furthermore, engineering efforts should prioritize the integration of predictive machine learning algorithms directly into the data modeling layer to calculate individual customer churn probabilities before users enter the At Risk classification.
